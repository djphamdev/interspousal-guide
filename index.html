Interspousal Transfer Exclusion Guide

Fixing the 50% improper reassessment after adding a spouse to a deed in LA County

R&T Code §63 Exclusion Applies

The Situation

Your friend got married, his wife owned a home, and they updated the deed in LA County to add him. The county's TITAN system treated this as a sale and reassessed 50% of the property to fair market value — triggering a massive property tax increase.

The good news: This was a mistake. Under California law, interspousal transfers are statutorily excluded from reassessment.

Why the 50% Reassessment Happened

Normal Rule

Adding someone to a deed = "change of ownership" → reassessment of transferred interest (50%) to current fair market value.

Spousal Exception

Transfers between spouses are explicitly excluded from "change of ownership" under R&T Code §63. The Prop 13 base year value stays intact.

What Went Wrong

  • PCOR (BOE-502-A) wasn't filed or didn't claim the exclusion
  • TITAN system/analyst didn't recognize the exemption
  • Issued supplemental/escape assessment as if it were a regular sale

Can This Be Fixed 5 Years Later?

Yes, generally — but timing mechanics matter.

The Rules

  • Claim must be filed within 3 years of transfer OR before transfer to a third party (whichever is earlier)
  • If a Notice of Supplemental/Escape Assessment was mailed after those deadlines → additional 6 months from notice date to file
  • If filed after 3 years but property hasn't been sold to a third party: exclusion still granted, but applies prospectively from lien date of claim year (not retroactive to original transfer)

At 5 Years Out — Still Viable If:

  • They never received a supplemental/escape notice until recently → may be within 6-month window for full retroactive relief
  • Escape assessment rules let Assessor reopen/correct prior rolls up to 4 years normally, 8 years if Change in Ownership Statement wasn't filed
  • Assessor can cancel/reduce escape assessments and issue refunds/credits for overpaid taxes + interest

Action Plan — What to Do Now

1

Gather Documents

  • Recorded deed adding spouse to title
  • Preliminary Change of Ownership Report (PCOR / BOE-502-A) if available
  • Marriage certificate (proof of marital status at time of transfer)
  • All supplemental/escape assessment notices and tax bills showing 50% FMV reassessment
2

Contact LA County Assessor's Office

Say this: "This was an interspousal transfer under R&T Code §63; we believe it was mistakenly treated as a sale and reassessed 50% to FMV."

Ask for:

  • Review/cancellation of supplemental/escape assessment based on interspousal exclusion
  • What form(s) they want: usually a corrected PCOR and/or written claim for interspousal transfer exclusion
3

File the Claim

Depending on Assessor's response, submit:

  • Corrected BOE-502-A (PCOR) with spousal exclusion box checked
  • Copy of deed + marriage certificate
  • Specific claim for reassessment exclusion referencing §63
4

If Assessor Won't Cooperate — Appeal

File Application for Changed Assessment with the Assessment Appeals Board (form BOE-305-AH) within 60 days of the mailing date on the escape/supplemental notice or tax bill.

Bring: deed, PCOR, marriage proof, clear timeline of transfer date and notice receipt.

Key Forms & References

BOE-502-A

Preliminary Change of Ownership Report (PCOR) — claim the spousal exclusion here

BOE-305-AH

Application for Changed Assessment — Assessment Appeals Board

R&T Code §63

Statutory authority: interspousal transfers excluded from "change of ownership"

Likelihood of Success

On the law: Very strong — §63 is clear
On timing: Still viable at 5 years — especially with escape assessment
Best case: Full cancellation + refunds + interest
Worst case: Prospective relief only — future taxes reduced, past years not refunded

Sample Letter to LA County Assessor

To: Los Angeles County Assessor
Re: Interspousal Transfer Exclusion Claim — APN [parcel number]

Dear Assessor,

On [date], a deed was recorded adding [spouse name] to the title of the property at [address] (APN: [number]). This transfer was an interspousal transfer between married spouses, which is excluded from "change of ownership" under Revenue & Taxation Code §63.

However, the Assessor's system (TITAN) appears to have treated this as a sale, resulting in a 50% reassessment to fair market value and subsequent supplemental/escape assessments.

We request:
1. Review and cancellation of the supplemental/escape assessment(s) issued for this transfer
2. Application of the interspousal exclusion retroactive to the original transfer date
3. Refund/credit of all overpaid property taxes plus statutory interest

Enclosed: Recorded deed, marriage certificate, PCOR (BOE-502-A), and all assessment notices.

We are happy to provide any additional documentation needed.

Sincerely,
[Names]
